# Current research plan: psychological expression in final statements

Updated 7 November 2026 following the owner's clarification of the original aim.
This is a substantive reorientation, not a newly completed psychological study.

## Aim and research question
Understand the expressed emotional experience, relational concerns and meaning
making of people facing execution. Study psychological expression through
language; do not claim direct access to private experience, diagnose disorders,
establish actual guilt, or infer sincerity from last statements alone.

**Main question:** How do people facing execution express emotions, maintain
relationships, and make sense of death and their own situation in their final
statements?

**Initial focused question:** How do affection, gratitude and spiritual language
coexist with explicit self-described emotional states and stances toward death
and execution? Do these expressions address the speaker, other people, or both?

Positive relational language, emotional calm, acceptance of mortality, procedural
readiness and approval of execution are distinct constructs. They may coexist
or diverge. Do not define any one as proof of the others. Texts can support
descriptive combinations and contextual interpretations, not causal claims that
religion or affection reduces death anxiety.

## Minimal contextual extension, not replacement labels
Retain original 1/0/u labels and all frozen decisions. Pilot supplementary
evidence notes before creating a revised codebook or expanding annotation.

1. **Explicit affect or self-description:** quote exact wording and identify the
   experiencer. Separate the speaker's current feeling, another person's feeling,
   a wish/instruction, remembered experience, negation and religious quotation.
   I hope you find peace is not I am at peace. Not expressed is not not felt.
2. **Death and execution stance:** distinguish expressed calm/fear/resistance or
   acceptance of mortality from criticism/approval of execution. Treat procedure
   readiness and statement closure separately. Keep coexistence and uncertainty.
3. **Addressee and textual function:** record explicit addressees and observable
   acts such as affection, consolation, farewell, request and protest. Do not
   upgrade these to inferred psychological functions such as anxiety regulation
   or impression management without additional evidence.

Every note needs exact source offsets, complete context, explicit evidence,
alternative reading and a limit on inference. Editorial crying is an observer
annotation, not the speaker's self-report or proof of fear. Figurative home,
religious afterlife and ordinary family reunion cannot be silently equated.

## Small first pilot
Six previously discussed records were purposively chosen for contrasting
interpretive issues: 138, 330, 407, 413, 433 and 461. Selection was informed by
known texts and labels. It is not random, representative, independent or
exhaustive. AI notes in reports/psychological_expression_pilot_v1.json are
proposals for analysis, not six new owner-approved psychological labels.

The pilot establishes only that the current materials can support the proposed
distinctions. It does not establish their frequency, a population association,
fear absence, coping success or a final thematic finding. Next read the entire
50-record development set systematically, record counterexamples and revise
definitions before choosing any further sample. Do not add a bulk labeling
request until the framework and a manageable review budget are agreed.

| Execution | Evidence anchor | Contextual observation and boundary |

| 413 | I hope that someday you can find peace | Peace is wished for others, not asserted as the speaker's current feeling; apology and spiritual language need separate reading |
| 433 | I only have love in my heart; I am ready to go home | Explicit love does not establish lack of fear; home does not independently establish a religious afterlife |
| 461 | (crying); I am ready to go Warden | Observer-recorded crying and procedural readiness coexist; do not infer fear or acceptance, or mix editorial notes into prepared speech without disclosure |

Full statements, exact evidence offsets, source hashes, frozen labels and
alternative-reading limits accompany these anchors in the JSON pilot. No
co-occurrence rate or population frequency is computed from this selection.

## Bounded next steps and outputs
1. Establish the current framework and evidence pilot (this update). Pause new
   classifier features and additional procedural notebooks. Preserve historical
   methods and audit defects; pausing does not repair those defects.
2. Extend contextual reading across the existing 50, with evidence and negative
   cases, and obtain psychological/domain feedback on construct definitions.
   This remains exploratory framework development, not blind validation.
3. Once definitions stabilize, decide a bounded additional annotation or full
   corpus design. Use document-level denominators, separate 0/u, disclose missing
   statements and length/period differences. Do not use the equal-decade 50 as a
   corpus prevalence estimate or treat multiple spans as independent people.

A useful next research result is a small contextual account showing how
relational language and death/execution stance can differ, backed by evidence
and counterexamples. Automation is optional support. One final statement per
person cannot establish within-person emotional change over time. Publication
readiness and psychological validity remain unproved.
