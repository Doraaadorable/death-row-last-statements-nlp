# Related work

Published studies of Texas last statements code the texts by hand. They differ in how they define constructs and how they report reliability, which is what the measurement study builds on.

| Study | Material | Coding and reliability | Relevance here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schuck & Ward (2008) | 283 statements | Sequential and micro-level discourse analysis | Separates apology from guilt; shows accepted guilt alongside opposition to the death penalty |
| Eaton & Theuer (2009) | 305 statements, executions 1982–2007 | Two raters, present/absent/unclear; disagreements coded unclear; kappa .89–.99 | Source of the construct boundaries: apology, remorse, empathy, forgiveness, responsibility, guilt, innocence, accident and excuse are separate variables. Apology 32.5%, innocence 16.4% |
| Smith (2018) | 429 oral statements (537 records), 270 religious | Open coding with two external coders; agreement above 90%, then consensus | Religious-coping functions, several per statement |
| Smith (2019) | 115 statements with forgiveness language | Same procedure; six non-exclusive categories | Separates forgiveness from reconciliation |
| Smith (2020) | 269 religious statements | Same record window as Smith (2018) | Religious functions by race and ethnicity |
| Malaga & Delgado (2020) | 61 statements, 2011–2017 | Descriptive phenomenological analysis | Experiential themes and addressees |
| Zhuo & Chun (2026) | 198 statements of inmates classified as White, 1984–2021 | Iterative interpretive reading; no reliability statistic | 855 emotional and 273 evaluative expressions; apology to different audiences, forgiveness with admonition, responsibility with judicial criticism |
| Pargament, Feuille & Burdzy (2011) | Brief RCOPE review | Questionnaire psychometrics | Theory of positive and negative religious coping |

## What this means for the measurement study

1. **Construct boundaries come from Eaton & Theuer.** Their separate variables for apology, remorse and empathy, and for innocence, accident and excuse, are the distinctions most likely to trip an automated measure. The [codebook](../study/measurement_validity/CODEBOOK.md) follows them, with three documented differences.
2. **Reliability benchmark.** Only Eaton & Theuer report chance-corrected agreement (kappa .89–.99). Smith reports percent agreement; the interpretive studies report none. The measurement study reports kappa with intervals before adjudication.
3. **Rates are not directly comparable.** Each study uses a different period, denominator and unit (statements, expressions or people), and this project's apology definition is narrower than Eaton & Theuer's. Prevalence in the study sample is reported descriptively.

The full reading notes, with page references and the comparison with the earlier close reading, are in [archive/close_reading/LITERATURE_COMPARISON.md](../archive/close_reading/LITERATURE_COMPARISON.md).
